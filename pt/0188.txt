about a mile on the N. E. ſide of Tabago, and a good
Channel between them : this Iſland hath no Name
that ever I heard.

While we lay at Tabago we had like to have had a
ſcurvy trick plaid us by a pretended Merchant from
Panama, who came, as by ſtealth, to traffick with us
privately ; a thing common enough with the Spaniſh
Merchants, both in the North and South Seas, notwith-
ſtanding the ſeverere Prohibitions of the Governours;
who yet ſometimes connive at it, and will even trade
with the Privateers themſelves. Our Merchant was
by agreement to bring out his Bark laden with Goods
in the night, and we to go and anchor at the South
of Perico. Out he came, with a Fireſhip inſtead of a
Bark, and approached very near, haling us with the
Watch-word we had agreed upon. We ſuſpecting the
worſt, call'd to them to come to an anchor, and upon
